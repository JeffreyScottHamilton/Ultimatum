# Writing the words

Small-business sites usually fail on words, not design. Draft copy here, in the owner's voice from `brand/brand.md`, and pass it to `ultimatum-launch:website` in the skill args. The owner approves every claim.

## Rules

- Write the way customers talk. Use the words from intake ("what did your last three customers ask first?"), reviews and enquiry emails.
- Lead with the result for the visitor, then how, then who you are.
- Specific beats impressive: "Boiler repairs in Leeds, usually the same week" says more than "quality heating solutions".
- One idea per sentence. Short paragraphs. Headings a skimmer can follow.
- Every claim is true and can be shown: years in business, number of jobs, awards, licences. No "best in town" unless a real award says so.
- Never write fake reviews, invented numbers or invented client names.

## Headline patterns

Pick one, fill it with the owner's real details, and write three versions for the owner to choose from.

| Pattern | Shape | Example (fictional) |
|---|---|---|
| What, where, for whom | [Service] in [place] for [people] | Family dentistry in Northgate, open Saturdays |
| Result | [Result] without [pain] | A tidy garden all summer, without giving up your weekends |
| Proof first | [Number or fact] [service] | 400 kitchens fitted across the valley since 2012 |
| Invitation | [Action] [benefit] | Book a cut in two minutes, pay in the chair |

Under the headline: one supporting line that answers "why you?", then the main button.

## Button wording

Use the action and the object, the same words everywhere.

| Weak | Better |
|---|---|
| Submit | Send my quote request |
| Click here | Book a table |
| Learn more | See treatment prices |
| Contact | Call now: 01234 567 890 (as a tap link) |

## Service page template

1. **Heading:** the service in the customer's words, plus the place if local.
2. **Who it is for:** one or two lines naming the situation.
3. **What is included:** a short list.
4. **How it works:** three or four numbered steps, from first contact to done.
5. **Price:** a price, a "from" price or "free quote", as the owner chooses.
6. **Proof:** one review or one job photo about this service.
7. **Questions:** three to five real ones from customers, with short answers.
8. **Action:** the button again.

## About page template

- Who: names and real photos.
- Why: the reason the business started, in two or three sentences, in the owner's words.
- How: what the business does differently that a customer would notice.
- Credentials: licences, training, memberships, years.
- Local roots (if local): the area, community links.
- Action: the button.

Interview the owner for this with questions asked one at a time in chat: "How did you start?", "What do customers thank you for most?", "What do you refuse to do that others do?"

## Review and proof lines

- Quote reviews exactly; trim only with "…", never change meaning. Show the first name and town or the source, as the reviewer allowed.
- Pick reviews that mention a specific worry being solved (on time, clean, explained the price) over general praise.
- Pair a number with its source: "4.9 from 120 reviews on [platform]" only if it is current; check it on the day of launch.

## Meta titles and descriptions

- Title: service, place, business name (rule of thumb: under about 60 characters).
- Description: what you offer, the proof, the action (about 155 characters).
- Each page gets its own; no duplicates.

- [ ] Read aloud once: no jargon the customer would not use.
