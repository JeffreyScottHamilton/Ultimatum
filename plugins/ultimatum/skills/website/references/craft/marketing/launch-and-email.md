# Launch and email

Read when the site, a new service or a new offer is about to go public, or when the owner wants the first automated emails (welcome, after enquiry, after a job). The technical go-live list is `../../launch-checklist.md`; this file is about getting attention and keeping it. Every send, post and listing change needs the owner's yes.

## 1. Ready to announce?

Announce only when all of these hold:

- [ ] `launch-checklist.md` done: forms reach the inbox, payments in live mode only if approved, redirects in place.
- [ ] Tracking proven with a test conversion (`measure.md` §3), so the launch can be measured.
- [ ] The main page passes `ult market page` with no fails and the owner approved every claim.
- [ ] Someone can answer enquiries within the promised time during launch week.
- [ ] The Business Profile and main directories show the same name, address, phone, hours and site link.

## 2. Channels, in order of control

| Kind | Examples | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| **Owned:** you control it | the site, the email list, the customer database, printed material | keeps working after launch; build these first |
| **Borrowed:** someone else's audience, by agreement | local press, a partner business's newsletter, a podcast, a trade association, a local group admin who shares it | trust from a known voice; ask, don't spam |
| **Rented:** a platform's audience under its rules | social accounts, marketplaces, review sites, directories, launch sites | reach, but the rules and reach change without notice; always point back to owned |

Every borrowed or rented mention should send people to an owned place: the site, with a way to join the list or book.

## 3. Launch in steps

1. **Friends and past customers first** (a week before): a personal email or message from the owner with the new site link, asking for one honest piece of feedback. Fix what they find.
2. **Ask for reviews** from recent happy customers, with the review link, no reward and no filtering to only happy ones.
3. **Launch day:** email the list and add a Business Profile post. One clear thing to do in each: book, see the new menu, claim the opening offer. Social posts are not part of this guide yet; leave them to the owner.
4. **Borrowed reach in week one:** one personal pitch each to two or three local or trade outlets or partners, with a real story (a new service the area lacked, an anniversary, a local hire), a photo, and a contact. No mass press releases.
5. **Launch sites and directories** (for software, products or creators): only those where the buyers actually look. Prepare the description, images and the first comment in advance; be present to answer on the day; never ask for upvotes in bulk or swap them.
6. **Two weeks later:** report results (`measure.md` §5) and pick one follow-up: more reviews, a seasonal offer, a partner.

For changes after launch (new service, new hours, new prices): a short "what's new" on the site, one email, one post, and update every listing that shows the changed fact.

## 4. Email basics

**Permission.** Email only people who asked: they ticked a clear box or signed up for the lead offer, and were told what they'd get. Buying lists is out. Existing-customer exemptions vary by country (UK and EU "soft opt-in", US CAN-SPAM opt-out rules): check before relying, and let the owner decide with that in mind. Every marketing email has the sender's name, a postal address and a one-click unsubscribe that works immediately.

**Delivery.** Send from the owner's own domain with SPF, DKIM and a DMARC record set up (the host or email tool shows how; the owner adds the DNS records). Since 2024 Gmail and Yahoo require these, plus one-click unsubscribe and a low spam-complaint rate, for bulk senders: check before relying on the exact thresholds.

**One job per email.** One message, one link or button, the owner's real name as sender, a subject that says what's inside, and preview text that adds to it.

## 5. The first sequences

Write them in the owner's voice, short, plain text or nearly plain. Each email: the reason it's sent, one useful thing, one action.

**After a lead-offer sign-up** (over about two weeks):

| When | Job |
|---|---|
| Right away | Deliver what was promised, say what's next and how often you'll write |
| Day 2 | The most common mistake people make with the problem, and how to avoid it |
| Day 5 | A real story: a customer like them, what they did, the result (with permission) |
| Day 9 | Answer the top worry: price, timing, trust |
| Day 14 | A direct invitation: book, quote, call, with an easy way to reply |

Stop the sequence as soon as they book or buy. After it ends, move them to the normal newsletter only if they agreed to it.

**After an enquiry:** an instant confirmation saying when they'll hear back and what to prepare; a follow-up if no reply within two working days.

**After a job or purchase:** a thank-you with care instructions or next steps; about a week later, a review request with the direct link; a reminder when the service is due again (boiler service, dental check, seasonal clean), only if they agreed.

**Measure:** opens are unreliable (privacy features inflate them); judge by clicks, replies, bookings and unsubscribes. An unsubscribe rate over about 1% per email, or any spam complaints trending up, means the list or the content is off.
