# Playbooks by business type

Pick the row that matches the intake answer, then use its section for the brief, the page list and the hand-off notes to `ultimatum-launch:website`. Everything here is a rule of thumb; the owner's real situation wins.

| Type | Main action | Must-have pages | Best proof | Usual video |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Restaurant, cafe, bakery | Reserve, order, or visit | Home, Menu, Visit (hours, map), About | Food photos, review quotes | Brand Film (the kitchen, the regulars) |
| Salon, barber, spa | Book online | Home, Services and prices, Team, Book | Before-and-after, reviews | Brand Film or a short Motion price list |
| Clinic, dentist, therapist | Book or call | Home, Treatments, Team and credentials, Book, Patient info | Credentials, reviews where allowed | Brand Film (a calm welcome), no outcome claims |
| Trades (plumber, electrician, builder, landscaper) | Call or request a quote | Home, Services, Areas, Work, Quote | Photos of real jobs, licence and insurance, reviews | Motion (how it works) or a short job-site film |
| Consultant, agency, coach | Book a discovery call | Home, Services or Offers, Case studies, About, Contact | Named results with permission, client logos | Brand Film (the founder) |
| Portfolio, photographer, creator | Enquire or follow | Work, About, Contact (often one page) | The work itself | The work as the reel; usually no extra film |
| Studio, gym, classes | Book a class or a free trial | Home, Classes and timetable, Pricing, Instructors, Book | Member stories, class photos | Brand Film (a class in motion) |

## Restaurant, cafe, bakery

- Put the menu on the page as text, not only a PDF or a photo: it loads faster on a phone, can be read aloud and can be found by search.
- Hours, address, phone and a directions link sit in the first screen on mobile. Holiday hours updated on the site and the listing at the same time.
- Allergens and dietary marks only as the owner supplies them; never guess.
- If ordering or reservations go through a third-party service, link or embed it and test it on a phone. Its fees and terms: check before relying.
- Common mistakes: an out-of-date menu, a menu PDF that is several megabytes, hours that differ from the listing.

## Salon, barber, spa

- A service list with durations and "from" prices the owner confirms, grouped the way customers think (cut, colour, treatments).
- Team page with each person's specialty and a book button per person if the booking tool supports it.
- Cancellation and deposit policy near the book button, in plain words.
- Before-and-after photos only with the client's permission.
- Common mistakes: a booking link buried in the menu, prices that disagree with the booking tool.

## Clinic, dentist, therapist, other health

- Health claims are high risk: no promised outcomes, no "cure", no "painless" unless the owner can support it and local rules allow. The compliance reviewer checks every line.
- Show registration or licence details as the profession requires (the owner supplies them).
- A "first visit" section: what to bring, how long, cost or insurance basics, parking and access.
- Contact forms must not invite people to send medical details. Ask for name, contact and a preferred time only, and say so.
- Reviews and testimonials can be restricted for some professions in some places: check before relying.
- Common mistakes: stock photos of smiling models, a form that asks for symptoms.

## Trades

- The phone number and a "Get a quote" button in the header on every page; many visitors are in a hurry.
- A list of towns or a map of the service area; one page per area only with real local jobs to show.
- Licence, insurance and any trade body membership with numbers the owner supplies.
- Job photos with a one-line story (problem, fix, town). Real photos beat stock by a wide margin.
- A quote form that asks for the job type, the town, a photo upload if possible, and the best time to call.
- Emergency or same-day service only if the owner really offers it, with real hours.
- Common mistakes: no area listed, a quote form with ten fields, no photos of actual work.

## Consultant, agency, coach

- One clear offer per page, written as the problem the client has and the result they get, not a list of methods.
- Case studies: situation, what was done, the result in numbers if true and approved by the client.
- The action is a short call; use a scheduling link and say what happens on the call and how long it takes.
- An optional free resource (a checklist or short guide) to collect emails, with a clear privacy note.
- Common mistakes: vague headlines ("empowering growth"), no prices or starting points at all, no faces.

## Portfolio, photographer, creator

- The work comes first, large and fast: compress images, use modern formats, lazy-load below the first screen.
- Group work into a few clear sets; each piece gets a title and one line of context.
- Contact with what to include in an enquiry (date, location, budget range) so replies are useful.
- For creators: links to the channels that matter, a short "work with me" section, and a media kit if they sell sponsorships.
- Common mistakes: a slow gallery, no way to contact, too many pieces of uneven quality.

## Studio, gym, classes

- Timetable as real text that is easy to update, or the booking tool's live embed.
- A clear first step: free trial, intro offer or drop-in price, with the terms the owner confirms.
- Instructor bios with real photos.
- Common mistakes: an outdated timetable image, membership terms hidden until checkout.

## Hand-off notes

When calling `ultimatum-launch:website`, paste: the type, the main action and its exact button wording, the page list from the table, the proof the owner has, the type's common mistakes to avoid, and any compliance limits (health, licences, reviews).
