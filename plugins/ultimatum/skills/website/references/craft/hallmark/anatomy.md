# Anatomy: what a page is built around, by the visitor's job

Read at the shape step, once the prompt's job is named. It sets where the action lives, which sections belong to the category and how the page ends. The theme dresses the anatomy and the shape orders it; neither replaces it. The usual generated page (text beside a picture, a figure row, three steps, an email band) is how software pages look and almost nothing else.

## Rules for every job

- The nav carries the job's own action word (Book, Tickets, Shop, Listen, Start free, Call, Get a quote). The hero shows the thing itself (the room, the cover, the dates, the screen). Proof arrives in the category's own form, never a generic band of numbers.
- **When the prompt gives none of a section's content,** build a sample the user replaces, marked in the handoff: an example issue with its parts, dishes typical of the cuisine, three candles named plainly, placeholder track titles. A sample shows the shape of the real thing and never states a fact a visitor could take as true (a price, a date, a person's name, a count, a review).
- A section whose whole content would be facts nobody gave (a lineup, a tour) is one line and a text slot, or it waits. Practical facts the job wants and the prompt lacks (hours, address, dates) share the page's two missing-fact slots (`craft-copy.md` § Missing facts).

## Jobs from the source

**Subscribe (newsletter, waitlist, podcast).** Action in the hero, often the hero itself: name, one line, the email field; a podcast sets the latest episode with its play control. Sections: a sample or the latest issue as it arrives, who writes it, the archive as an Index of issues or episodes with dates, a reader count only if given and set like a circulation figure. Ends on a second field under the archive, or the archive. The one job whose close may be an email field.

**Book (restaurant, class, clinic, studio).** Action: a picker (party, date, time) from nav and hero, or the next free times as buttons; repeated beside the menu or classes, never only at the foot. Sections: the room or food as a photograph with little head; the menu (a Switcher for lunch and dinner, or rows with prices); the timetable as a Schedule for classes; how to find it; hours and address as their own section. Ends on hours, address and the booking again. A fine room may have almost nothing: one photo, the menu, the booking.

**Visit (shop, gallery, venue, place).** Action: today's hours and the address in nav or hero; tickets or donate only if given. Sections: what is on now as the lead picture; what is coming as dated rows (Index or Schedule, with this-week and next-week switch); for a shop the shelves (a Rail or Grid of the prompt's own things); getting there. Ends on visit information and address, or the list for what is on next.

**Attend (event, festival, race, conference).** Action: tickets in the nav from the first screen; date and place are the headline. Sections: lineup or speakers (Index or dense Grid, names large); the days as a Schedule; ticket tiers with prices; venue and travel; questions. Figures only when given. Ends on tickets again, the questions, or the list for the next edition.

**Buy (physical product, small shop).** Action: the product with price and options (an Offer), often the hero; a buy action after the sections that earn it, or a sticky bar with the price. Sections: the range as tiles or a Rail with prices; the product in use, close; how it is made; delivery and guarantee as plain facts; reviews only if given. Ends on the offer again, new arrivals or the guarantee. An email field belongs in the footer, not as the close.

**Try (app, game, desktop tool).** Action: store or download buttons right under the head, one per platform named. Sections: key art, trailer or main screen captured from the product (`imagery.md` § Product); features as named moments shown in screens (a Pinned run or Switcher), not icon tiles; a before and after; platforms. Ends on the download beside one last screen. Games are short: five or six blocks is a full page.

**Sign up (software, API, developer tool).** Action: start free in nav and hero with its terms in one line; for a developer tool the install command may be the action. Sections: the product at work (captured or built), named moments, a code sample or integration, the pricing table with its period switch, questions. Ends on the hero's action in a short band, or the code sample. Never a newsletter.

**Hire (studio, freelancer, architect).** Action: a plain contact (email, phone, "Talk to us") in nav and once after the work; no form needed. Sections: the work as the page, a list or Index of projects (name, client, year, place) with pictures, one opened as a case; what the studio does as a short list; where it is. Ends on contact details, per office.

**Join (club, community, membership).** Action: join or "your first visit" in the hero. Sections: what a session is like, shown; when and where as a Schedule or list of places; membership options as rows with prices; first-visit guidance; people only in the prompt's own pictures. Ends on the first visit, the options, or a sign-up field.

**Listen (band, film, podcast, publication).** Action: the new release or current issue with listen, watch or order buttons side by side. Sections: the record, film or issue (cover, tracks or contents as an Index); dates as rows with ticket links; earlier work as covers in a Rail or Grid; videos. Ends on dates, the catalogue or the list. With no release in the prompt the act leads instead (players, how it sounds described plainly, where it plays) and the job is booking them: the close is the enquiry.

## How pages end

In the order real sites use them: the hero's action again in a short band; practical details (hours, address, contacts); more of the catalogue; a short trust tail (a guarantee, press, awards the prompt gives); a mailing list, for things that recur. The last is one close in five, never the default (gate 85).

## Added for Ultimatum: local trades and apps

These two are not in the source and follow the same pattern. See `../../by-business-type.md` for per-trade section lists and `../../pages-and-seo.md` for trust signals and local SEO.

**Call or get a quote (plumber, electrician, cleaner, landscaper, repair).** Action: a tap-to-call link and a short quote form, both in the nav and hero; a sticky call bar on phones. Sections: the services as rows with a starting price or "from" range only if given; the area served as a map or list of towns; real jobs as photos of the owner's own work; licences, insurance and reviews exactly as the owner states them; how a quote works in one honest line. Ends on the phone number and the form again, with hours and the service area.

**Use the product after sign-in (dashboard, settings, checkout).** This is an app screen, not a marketing page; follow `flow-branches.md` § App screens (rules for tools). Marketing anatomy applies to the public pages (pricing, sign-up) only; checkout shows the order, the price and the one pay action with nothing else competing.
