# Tickets and sign-ups

Set up the sign-up so it is easy to say yes, clear about what happens next, and tested before it goes live. Fees, payout timing and platform terms are **check before relying**. Opening paid sales needs the organiser's yes.

## Free, paid or donation

| Model | Good for | Watch |
|---|---|---|
| Free with sign-up | Meetups, talks, openings, webinars | Free sign-ups miss more often; send reminders and consider a waitlist |
| Paid, one price | Workshops, classes, shows | Price covers costs, the platform fee and the organiser's time |
| Paid with tiers | Conferences, shows with seating | Keep tiers few and their difference obvious |
| Pay what you can or donation | Community events, fundraisers | Show a suggested amount; say where money goes |

If the organiser is unsure about price, list the costs (venue, materials, food, fees, helpers) and divide by a realistic attendance, not full capacity. Mark the result as a guess until they confirm.

## Early-bird and tiers

- An early-bird price has a real end date stated on the page. Close it on that date.
- Tier names say what is different: "Standard", "With materials kit", "Supporter (helps fund a free place)".
- Group or companion tickets help events people attend with a friend.
- Never show a "was" price that was never charged.

## Capacity and waitlist

- Set the limit to what the venue, the host and the materials can handle. The venue's legal or safe capacity is **check locally** with the venue.
- When full, switch to a waitlist that collects name and email only.
- Decide how places are offered from the waitlist: first come, first served, with a deadline to accept.
- For free events, some organisers allow a little over capacity because of no-shows; only do this if the venue limit allows it and the organiser agrees.

## Refund and transfer policy, in plain words

Write three or four sentences the buyer can understand. Fictional example:

> If you cannot come, you can give your place to a friend: just reply to your confirmation email with their name. Refunds are available until 7 days before the workshop. After that, we cannot refund, but you can move to a later date if there is space. If we cancel, you get a full refund.

- State the deadline as a date, not only "7 days before".
- Say what happens if the organiser cancels or moves the event.
- Consumer rules on refunds differ by place: **check locally**, and have the organiser decide.
- The same policy appears on the event page, at checkout and in the confirmation.

## The form

Ask only what the event needs.

| Ask | Why |
|---|---|
| Name and email | Confirmation and reminders |
| Ticket type and quantity | Capacity |
| Access needs (optional, free text) | So the organiser can prepare |
| Dietary needs, only if food is served | Catering |
| Marketing consent, unticked by default | Permission to email about future events |
| Photo consent, if photos will be taken | Recorded choice, not assumed |

Do not ask for: date of birth, home address, phone number or job details unless the event truly needs them; any ID numbers; anything "nice to know". Each extra field lowers sign-ups and adds data to protect.

## Messages

| Message | When | Contents |
|---|---|---|
| Confirmation | Immediately | What, when, where (or link), what to bring, refund and transfer line, calendar file, contact |
| Reminder 1 | About a week before | Same details, anything new, how to give the place to someone else |
| Reminder 2 | The day before | Start time, address or link, arrival and access details, contact for the day |
| Waitlist offer | When a place opens | Deadline to accept, the button |
| Cancellation or change | As soon as known | What changed, options, refund steps |

Write them in the brand voice; short, practical, one button where there is an action. Sending to a list needs the organiser's yes.

## End-to-end test

Before announcing, with the tool in test mode or with a real low-value purchase refunded afterwards (organiser's yes):

- [ ] Open the event page on a phone; the button leads to the sign-up.
- [ ] Complete a sign-up or purchase as a new buyer.
- [ ] Confirmation email arrives, reads well on a phone, and the calendar file opens with the right time zone.
- [ ] The attendee appears in the list and in the export.
- [ ] Capacity counts down; the waitlist switches on at the limit (test with a low limit, then reset).
- [ ] Refund or cancel the test; the refund message is correct.
- [ ] Discount or early-bird codes work and stop on their end date.
- [ ] Payouts go to the organiser's account (organiser checks; never share account details here).

Log each failure with `ult loop add "<finding>" --where "<step>" --blocker`.
