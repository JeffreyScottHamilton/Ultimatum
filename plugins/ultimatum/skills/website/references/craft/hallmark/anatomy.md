# Anatomy: what each page is built around, by the job a visitor came for

Read at the shape step, once the brief's job has a name. Anatomy fixes where the action sits, which sections the category expects and how the page closes. The theme dresses it and the shape sequences it; neither can replace it. The stock generated page (copy beside an image, a row of figures, three steps, an email strip) suits software marketing and almost nothing else.

## Rules for every job

- The nav names the job's own verb (Book, Tickets, Shop, Listen, Start free, Call, Get a quote). The first screen shows the real thing: whichever of room, cover, dates or screen applies. Proof takes the form the category uses, never a generic strip of numbers.
- **If the brief supplies nothing for a section,** build a sample the owner will replace and flag it in the handoff: a mock issue with all its parts, dishes common to the cuisine, three candles with plain names, filler track titles. A sample demonstrates the shape of the real thing and never asserts anything a visitor could believe (prices, dates, names of people, counts, reviews).
- When a section's whole body would be facts nobody supplied (a lineup, a tour), reduce it to one line and a text slot, or hold it back. Practical facts the job needs and the brief lacks (hours, address, dates) use the page's two shared missing-fact slots (`craft-copy.md` § Missing facts).

## Jobs from the source

Each entry gives the action, the sections and the close.

**Subscribe (newsletter, waitlist, podcast).** The action is the hero, or lives in it: a name, one line and the email field. A podcast leads with its newest episode and a play control. Sections: a sample or latest issue as it lands, the author, an archive set as an Index, each issue or episode dated, and a reader count only if supplied, set like a circulation figure. Close with a second field below the archive, or the archive itself. This is the only job that may end on an email field.

**Book (restaurant, class, clinic, studio).** The action is a picker (party size, date, time) in the nav and hero, or buttons for the next free times; show it again next to the menu or classes, not just at the foot. Sections: the room or food as a photograph with a light heading; the menu (a Switcher for lunch and dinner, or priced rows); a class timetable as a Schedule; directions; hours and address as their own section. Close with hours, address and booking again. A beautiful room can get by with almost nothing: one photo, the menu and the booking.

**Visit (shop, gallery, venue, place).** The action is today's hours and the address in nav or hero, with tickets or donation only if given. Sections: the current show as the lead picture; upcoming items as dated rows (an Index or Schedule with a toggle for this week and next); for a shop, the shelves as a Rail or Grid of its real stock; how to get there. Close with visit details and address, or a list for what comes next.

**Attend (event, festival, race, conference).** Tickets sit in the nav from the first screen, and date and place form the headline. Sections: lineup or speakers (an Index or dense Grid with large names); a Schedule for the days; priced ticket tiers; venue and travel; questions. Figures only if supplied. Close with tickets again, the questions, or a list for the next edition.

**Buy (physical product, small shop).** The action is the product with price and options (an Offer), often the hero, followed by a buy action once the sections have earned it, or a sticky bar showing the price. Sections: a priced Rail or set of tiles for the range; the product in use, shot close; how it is made; delivery and guarantee as plain facts; reviews only if supplied. Close by repeating the offer, or with new arrivals or the guarantee. Keep email capture in the footer; it is not the closer.

**Try (app, game, desktop tool).** Store or download buttons sit directly beneath the heading, one for each named platform. Sections: key art, a trailer or the main screen taken from the live product (`imagery.md` § Product); features shown as named moments on screens (a Pinned run or Switcher) instead of icon tiles; a before and after; supported platforms. Close with the download beside one more screen. Games are brief, and 5–6 blocks make a full page.

**Sign up (software, API, developer tool).** The action is start-free in nav and hero with its terms in a line; developer tools may make the install command the action. Sections: the product working (captured or built), named moments, a code sample or integration, a pricing table with a billing-period switch, questions. Close on a short band repeating the hero action, or on the code sample. A newsletter never closes it.

**Hire (studio, freelancer, architect).** The action is a plain way to reach them (email, phone or "Talk to us"), placed in the nav and once more after the work, with no form required. Sections: the work is the page, as a list or Index of projects with pictures (name, client, year, place), one of them opened up as a case study; a short list of what the studio does; location. Close with contact details, per office.

**Join (club, community, membership).** The hero action is join or "your first visit". Sections: a session shown as it feels; when and where, as a Schedule or list of places; membership options as priced rows; guidance for a first visit; people appear only in the owner's own pictures. Close with the first visit, the options or a sign-up field.

**Listen (band, film, podcast, publication).** The action is the newest release or current issue with listen, watch or order buttons beside each other. Sections: the record, film or issue (cover, tracks or contents as an Index); tour dates as rows linking to tickets; videos; the back catalogue as covers, in a Rail or a Grid. Close with dates, the catalogue or a list. With no release in the brief, the act leads instead (players, a plain description of the sound, where it plays) and the job becomes getting booked, so the page ends on the enquiry.

## How pages end

Closings on real sites usually run in this order: the hero action once more in a slim band, then practical details (hours, address, contacts), then more of the catalogue, then a brief trust tail (a guarantee, press mentions or awards the brief gives), then a mailing list where things recur. A mailing list should close about one page in five and is never the default (gate 85).

## Added for Ultimatum: local trades and apps

These two are not in the source and follow the same pattern. See `../../by-business-type.md` for per-trade section lists and `../../pages-and-seo.md` for trust signals and local SEO.

**Call or get a quote (plumber, electrician, cleaner, landscaper, repair).** Action: a tap-to-call link and a short quote form, both in the nav and hero; a sticky call bar on phones. Sections: the services as rows with a starting price or "from" range only if given; the area served as a map or list of towns; real jobs as photos of the owner's own work; licences, insurance and reviews exactly as the owner states them; how a quote works in one honest line. Ends on the phone number and the form again, with hours and the service area.

**Use the product after sign-in (dashboard, settings, checkout).** This is an app screen, not a marketing page; follow `flow-branches.md` § App screens (rules for tools). Marketing anatomy applies to the public pages (pricing, sign-up) only; checkout shows the order, the price and the one pay action with nothing else competing.
