# Anatomy: curved shading and textures

Read when a part is round (a drum, hopper, dial, coil) or has a textured surface.

## Curved parts shade smoothly

A cylinder or sphere uses a gradient across its width, built from the same four tones, not three or four flat bands. Stepped bands read as a faceted part. Place the lightest stop where the light direction meets the surface, and let the dark side pick up a little bounce so it does not go dead.

- Cylinder along an axis: a linear gradient across the width, light on the lit side.
- Cone or funnel: the same, with the stops following the slant.
- Sphere or dome: a radial gradient with its centre toward the light.
- A rim or lip gets its own thin ring with a lighter top.

## Textures end on edges or fade

| Texture | Rule |
|---|---|
| Knurling, ribs, fins | runs edge to edge, or ends at a clean ring |
| Tick marks | start and end on the scale's real ends; every tick the same length by rank |
| Grooves | a faint line that closes or leaves the part |
| Bolt dots | evenly spaced on a circle or a line, never random |
| Mesh, grille | clipped to the part, fading near its edge if it wraps round |

If a texture must stop in the open, fade its opacity to zero over a short distance instead of cutting.

## Coils and springs

Draw each turn as a front arc and a back arc, drawn in different layers, so the wire visibly passes in front of and behind the core. Keep the pitch even.

## Surface detail budget

Detail raises quality only up to the point where the figure stays readable at the card's real size. Look at it at 1x first. Detail that vanishes at 1x costs file size and gives nothing back; trim it or keep it for a print sheet (`print-sheets.md`).

## Contrast

Dark sides and edge lines must still be distinguishable from the ground. Check the figure's text and key lines at 4.5:1 and parts that carry meaning at 3:1 (`accessibility-and-limits.md`).
