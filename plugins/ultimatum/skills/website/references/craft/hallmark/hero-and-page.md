# The hero, the page as a whole, nav and footer

Read when picking a hero posture, and again when ordering sections and planning the close, nav and footer. Section rules live in `layout.md`; which sections a job needs is in `anatomy.md`. Postures also sit in `../../../../../data/craft/hallmark/hero-postures.csv`.

## The hero

The hero is the first screen at 1440×900 and at 1920×1080, and nothing beyond it (gate 44c). Choose a posture from the job, stamp it, and change it from one build to the next. A picture beside text (Settled, Stage) is the most frequent machine-made hero, so reach for it only if the picture is the best asset the brief offers.

| Posture | Height | What it holds |
|---|---|---|
| Settled | fills the screen below the nav | everything centred vertically; text across 6 columns, visual across the other 6 |
| Stage | fills the screen below the nav | text across 5 columns, visual across 7 at 60–75% of screen height (flip the sides if a poster-sized headline needs width); the visual may run off the outer edge |
| Banner | 50–60% of the screen | the first section appears above the fold; suited to reference, docs and utility pages |
| Ledge | fills the screen | a tall field on top with the headline resting at its base; at 1920 the headline hits its size cap before anything else grows |
| Corridor | fills the screen | a single centred column of 44ch at most, headline larger than normal |
| Poster | 92–100dvh | a full-bleed photo or canvas; content sits on its calmest area with air around it, placed low and left or centred, but never jammed into a corner |
| Counter | fills the screen below the nav | the action is the visual: a subscribe field beside the latest issue, booking times, a buy box, a download next to its screen, set large under a short headline |
| Index | fills the screen below the nav | the content is the visual: lineup, menu or project list leads, with a small headline above |

- **Local sites:** the first screen must show the phone number or booking action along with the place name. Counter and Poster are the usual postures for a shop or restaurant.
- **Vertical balance** (Settled, Stage, Banner, Corridor): the space beneath the content is 1.0–1.5 times the space above (44a), and the lowest content ends within 160px of the fold (79). Put leftover height into a bigger visual or bigger type. Copy never fills it.
- **A big visual:** 6–7 columns wide and at least 60% of screen height (520px at 1440×900), or a portrait-shaped object 75% tall on 4–5 columns, centred within 60px of the text's centre. Use a two-track grid (6fr 5fr or 5fr 7fr) with a 48–120px gap, which turns into a row gap below 60rem.
- **No visual:** enlarge the headline to display size across 8–12 columns, or use Banner so the first section begins by 640px.
- **The text:** a headline of seven words at most, one lead-size sentence, 1–2 actions. Below 60rem every posture shrinks to its content height (Poster may stay full-bleed). A working form belongs in a section or the close, not the default hero.

## The page

- **Anatomy comes from the job** (`anatomy.md`), which places the action (hero, sticky, repeated, closing) and lists the sections the category expects. The theme dresses that; it never replaces it.
- **Order follows the shape's argument** (`page-shapes.md`), never the hero, three features, call to action, footer default. After the hero, lead with the strongest real item and place each fact where it does its work.
- **Not every page needs steps, figures or a schedule.** Steps are for a real process; a figure row is for three numbers the brief states explicitly. Generated pages stack all of them by default, while a made page picks.
- **Variety.** No composition repeats between neighbouring sections; mirror a split once at most and never zigzag; product pages include at least one full-width visual; use 1–2 bands. A Statement, Steps, Figures or a closing Band is the lazy reach; when the last build used one, take a different route.
- **Balance.** After the hero, photographs may take 25–33% of the page, and two sections in a row never open with one. Between them, draw on the theme's own material: type, figures, rules, product, table, form.
- **Pace.** Follow a dense section (grid, table, bento, index) with a quiet one (statement, showcase) and finish on a real close.
- **Close with the job, not a form.** Finish on the thing the visitor arrived for, in that job's own shape (next times, buy box, store buttons, next date and address, newest issue), or on the final strong section plus the footer. Use an email field to close only when a mailing list truly belongs to the job or was requested (gate 85).
- **Footer:** 200–400px tall, with a wordmark or one closing line, the real links and the legal line. A letter, document or manifesto can end on a single line under a hairline. Local footers add name, address, phone and hours (`../../pages-and-seo.md`).
- **Nav:** a single line, 56–80px, sized to the real destinations. Vary it against the log. Options: edge-aligned (wordmark at one edge, 1–2 links or an action at the other); floating pill; masthead (large centred wordmark over a rule); slab (heavy full-width bar); a search-first bar showing a command pill; a banner stacked over a bar that retracts while scrolling; a three-part bar, only with four or more real destinations. Side rails appear only when a sticky object justifies one.
- **Footer shapes:** mast (large wordmark above links), statement (a big closing sentence with links under it), colophon block, newsletter-first (subscribe pages only), letter close (sign-off and date), index columns (reserve for a hub that has a real sitemap).

## Before shipping

Step through the page at 1440×900 a screen at a time. Does each screen hold something large enough to earn its place? If a screen has only a heading and a line, enlarge the visual, promote the line to a statement or merge two sections. Then blur the words: each section should keep a silhouette, and the page should alternate heavy and light. Automated checks cover width (77), heading scale (78), hero foot (79) and photo share (83); the rest is for your eye.
