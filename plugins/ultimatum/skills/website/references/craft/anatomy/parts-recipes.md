# Anatomy: recipes for common machine parts

Read when you are drawing a specific part (a base, a rail, a dial, a spring, a pipe run) and want the details that make it read as real.

Each recipe lists what the part is made of. Use all the pieces, in the part's quiet tone unless it is the subject (`craft-rules.md`).

## Parts on a flat body

| Part | Make it from |
|---|---|
| Base plate on feet | a slab with a bevelled top edge, four short feet with a flange, a faint groove inset from the edge, corner screws with rings, a label plate |
| Ruler strip and label plate | a thin slab with ticks (minor, medium, major by rank) that start and end on its ends, a zero mark, engraved text inside the plate with a margin |
| Seams and trim on a box | a faint line one step off the face tone, running edge to edge, with a screw or rivet row at even spacing |
| Rails, end stops, carriage | two rods or bars, a stop block at each end, a carriage whose underside sits on the rails and whose bushings surround them |
| Post, collar, clamp, thumb screw | a post on a foot, a split collar in back and front halves, a clamp lug, a screw with a knurled head |
| Drawer with pull and label holder | a face panel, a recess, a pull with two screws, a small card slot |
| Perforations, vents, dot grids | an even grid clipped to the panel, edge row smaller or faded, never cut mid-hole |

## Round and turned parts

| Part | Make it from |
|---|---|
| Rod out of a gland | a rod that continues inside the body, a gland ring and a nut at the exit, a faint shadow ring where it enters |
| Knurled knob with pointer | a cylinder with ribs edge to edge, a chamfered top, a pointer line from the centre that ends at the rim |
| Coil spring around a rod | arcs for the near and far halves, drawn in two layers with the rod between; even pitch |
| Dial gauge, face up | a bezel ring, a face ring, ticks on the face start and end on the scale arc, a needle from a hub, a crown on top |
| Turntable with degree ticks | a disc, a ring of ticks with a longer one every ten and a numeral, a pointer fixed to the base |
| Lamp, probe, finger | simple convex shapes with one lit accent; a probe has a pad that touches the subject |

## Think in meridians

For a turned part, describe its profile as radius against height, then revolve it. Bands (flat steps), ribs and rounded shoulders come out of that one profile, so edges stay concentric. Things on a round body (a screw on a collar, a label on a drum) follow the surface: their shape squeezes toward the silhouette and they hide past it.

## Pipe runs

- Choose waypoints first: where the pipe leaves, each bend, and where it arrives. Keep it away from other parts by the clearance rule.
- Contacts and junctions: a pipe meets a body at a port with a flange; two pipes meet at a tee with a clear collar, never overlapping tubes.
- Where two pipes cross in the view without touching, the front one draws over the back one with a small gap in the back one's outline.
- Wire and cable harness runs follow the structure, with clips at even spacing, and leave by a grommet.

## Screens and things on faces

Anything on a face is drawn in that face's skew, wholly inside its border with a margin. A screen has a bezel, a glass tone and content that does not touch the glass edge.

## Themes

A figure has a dark and a light theme. In the light one, use ink-weight edges and lighter faces; in the dark one, let faces carry the light. Same geometry, different tone tables.

See `craft-shading.md` for the gradients each round part needs.
